# 0003: The invoice tool targets U.S. service and B2B businesses that invoice, not hobby freelancers

Status: Accepted 2026-09-18

## Context

CDG's advantage is interchange plus with a published markup, which starts at $10,000 a month of card volume. Under that, CDG's Flat Rate is 3.50% + $0.30 online, which loses to Wave, Stripe Invoicing, and PayPal for a freelancer sending a few invoices a month. The invoice tool cannot be "free invoicing for everyone" and also be the on-ramp to CDG; those are different customers.

## Decision

- The invoice tool is positioned for U.S. service businesses, contractors, agencies, and B2B sellers who invoice clients and process, or expect to process, $10,000 a month or more.
- Sign-up is not gated by volume. Anyone can create and send invoices. The copy simply says who it is built for and why.
- Content that targets sub-$10K freelancers keeps recommending whatever is cheapest for them, even when that is not CDG.

## Reasons

- Positioning for the merchant CDG actually serves well keeps the product honest and keeps referrals from turning into refunds and complaints.
- Gating sign-up would cost trial usage that produces funnel data (see 0007) and would be unenforceable anyway.
- Honest content is the site's brand; recommending CDG to people it does not fit would undercut the reviews.

## Consequences

- Product copy says "for businesses that invoice clients" and names the volume band where CDG is the better deal.
- Roadmap features favor B2B invoicing: payment terms, PO numbers, partial payments, ACH, before consumer-style features.

## Revisit when

Sign-up data shows a clear cluster below $10K a month that converts to CDG anyway, or CDG changes its Flat Rate pricing.
