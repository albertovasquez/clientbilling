Processors sell simplicity or transparency. **Flat-rate** bundles costs into one percentage (sometimes plus a per-transaction fee). **Interchange-plus** shows network costs plus a processor markup.

## Flat-rate in one minute

You see something like “2.9% + $0.30” on many cards. Easy to forecast. Harder to know whether you overpay as volume grows or as debit mix increases.

## Interchange-plus in one minute

Your statement separates:

- Interchange (varies by card)
- Network assessments
- Processor markup (percent + cents)

CDG publishes mid-volume interchange-plus markups of **0.35% + $0.15 online** and **0.30% + $0.10 retail**, with interchange separate.

## Which tends to fit?

| Situation | Often better starting point |
| --- | --- |
| Low volume, need speed | Flat-rate simplicity |
| Rising volume, mixed cards | Interchange-plus transparency |
| Mostly card-present retail | Compare retail markups carefully |
| Heavy rewards / corporate cards | Model interchange; do not assume flat is cheaper |

## How to compare apples to apples

1. Export 1–3 months of card mix.
2. Estimate interchange (or ask providers for a cost analysis).
3. Add processor markup or flat rate.
4. Include monthly fees, PCI, chargeback, and gateway fees.
